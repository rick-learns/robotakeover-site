# RESULTS — Worldline Observatory

All numbers below come from harness output (`evidence/report.md`, generated by `harness/report.mjs` from `evidence/*.json`) and were copied into this file. Every experiment is reproducible with the commands in §21.

## 1. WORLD NAME
**Tellus** — as observed from the *Worldline Observatory* (`index.html`).

## 2. ONE-SENTENCE PREMISE
A 20-region planet whose climate, oxygen, life, people, technologies, energy systems, machines and AI lineages evolve only by rates computed from their own state, so that whether machines come to dominate — and what happens afterwards — is decided by physical, economic and institutional bottlenecks rather than by a script.

## 3. ART DIRECTION
"Planetary systems observatory meets far-future command interface": a dark instrument panel with hairline cyan/amber/violet, system monospace type, a slowly rotating orthographic globe whose every mark is state (land cover, farmland, built-up and machine land, night lights by energy per area coloured by machine share of work, cities, compute nodes, robot stocks, communication arcs with moving flow packets, orbital industry ring, off-world energy swarm, AI-lineage arcs whose angle/length/hue encode goal orientation/compute share/alignment). A legend lists each mapping. The epistemic zone (OBSERVED HISTORY → MODEL-CALIBRATED TRANSITION → SPECULATIVE FUTURE) is shown in the header, as background bands on the transition chart and as colours in the detector history, and the banner "HISTORICAL EVIDENCE ENDS. SIMULATION CONTINUES FROM MODEL ASSUMPTIONS." appears when the state-based boundary detector fires. Audio is fully procedural and state-driven (biosphere granules, urban drone, industrial ticks, communication chirps, compute blips, transition chords whose pitch derives from the takeover criteria, launch sweeps, replication motifs), capped at 24 voices with a compressor and a 14-voices/s limiter.

## 4. MODEL SUMMARY
One state vector (~890 numbers) evolves by `worldRules()` inside `// === WORLD RULES ===`, which only computes *rates*; the engine picks the time step from those rates (ε = 2 % relative change, looser for stiff variables) and integrates semi-implicitly, so deep time runs at up to 2 Myr/step and the AI transition at ~1 week/step. The rules never read the clock.

- **Planet:** stellar brightening, impactor depletion, radiogenic heat, continental growth, carbonate–silicate thermostat, ice–albedo (sigmoid: small caps stable, snowball runaway), oxygen from photosynthetic burial vs reduced-crust and volcanic sinks, radiative waste-heat balance, stochastic glacial excursions (Ornstein–Uhlenbeck).
- **Biosphere:** abiogenesis hazard ∝ prebiotic chemistry; biomass relaxes to a capacity set by light, nutrients (weathering), habitability and photosynthetic efficiency; evolving photosynthesis, complexity (ceiling set by O₂), diversity, neural capacity (selection pressure vs metabolic ceiling), cooperation; stochastic mass extinctions.
- **People (20 regions):** cumulative culture (fidelity × population vs forgetting); farming know-how (domesticates × climate stability × pressure, learning by doing, diffusion); Malthusian demography on total consumption with an Engel-curve labour split; demographic transition requiring income × medicine × literacy; a pronatalist subculture under selection vs secularisation; urbanisation, institutions (surplus & literacy vs conflict, inequality, complexity burden), scientific institutions (institutions × towns × printing), inequality, conflict, epidemics.
- **Technology:** nine domains, each advanced by induced research demand, craft/human/AI researchers, science multiplier, "ideas get harder" (φ = 0.3), level-coupled prerequisites, and diffusion by transport/communication.
- **Economy:** food + goods; physical work multiplied by useful mechanical power per worker (saturating); capital runs only with power; fossil fuel useful only with engines; depletion (fossil & metals, materials price index); clean energy up to land/fusion/heat ceilings; investment follows net returns through a portfolio that adjusts over ~2 years; task-based production where machines cover a fraction of tasks (and can do the rest only clumsily).
- **Computation & AI:** chip cost-performance ∝ A_comp¹⁰ (with a physical ceiling) and Koomey-like energy/FLOP; chip-fab and launch capacities are stocks that expand ≤ 30–35 %/yr; capability κ = log₁₀(training compute × algorithmic efficiency) per lineage; task coverage τ(κ) logistic; quality linear in κ above human level; inference floor; algorithms advance by a soft-minimum of researchers and experiment compute, with a ceiling; deployment when AI is cheaper than labour; autonomy when AI work outgrows human oversight capacity, limited by regulation; regulation reacts to incidents and is bounded by humans' economic leverage; delegation of capital when AI management returns more; AI-owned capital grows only through autonomous reinvestment.
- **Lineages (≤ 12):** replicator dynamics on compute share (capability edge, economies of scale weakened by off-world dispersal, human-customer preference for alignment, alignment tax), branching, merging, extinction, alignment training while humans control training, conflict ∝ goal divergence × scarcity × autonomy.
- **Space:** access needs transport + energy + materials; launches limited by launch capacity and fabs; in-space self-replication needs robotics × autonomy; bounded by accessible off-world material; orbital compute pays for chips and power.

## 6. TAKEOVER DEFINITION
Six machine shares computed each step from state (D1 cognitive-work value, D2 research effort, D3 AI-owned + AI-managed capital, D4 autonomy × √(D1·D5), D5 physical-work value, D6 autonomy × D1 × D5). **Machine dominance = D1–D4 ≥ 50 % continuously for ≥ 10 model years** (an observer counter; no rule reads it). "Full machine autonomy" additionally requires D5, D6 ≥ 50 %. There is no `aiWon` variable; the classification is recomputed from state every step and could revert. Declared before coding: D3/D4 contain autonomy, so NO AI AUTONOMY makes dominance unreachable *by definition*.

Reading the table:
- **No semiconductor scaling** is the only intervention that prevents AI entirely (compute stays below 1 % of the brain-compute scale; D1 = 0 in 20/20). Takeover is impossible. Human population stays ~10⁸. All 20 worlds end in a surface-economy collapse after ores run out, following ~1,200-year boom–bust cycles; this outcome is sensitive to integrator tolerance (audit round 2, N2).
- **No AI autonomy / no robotics** avoid "machine dominance" **by definition** (D3, D4 contain autonomy; D4, D5 need robots) — declared before coding. The informative content: without robots AI still performs ~100 % of cognitive work and research and controls ~90 % of capital, while humans keep 62 % of income and physical work (machine cognitive dominance while physically dependent — P10 confirmed); without autonomy the human income share stays 100 %.
- **No fossil fuels** delays industrial take-off by +563 yr and the AI transition by +291 yr (paired medians), but does not change the ending. **Severe energy constraint** delays both by ~150 yr; total energy capture at the horizon is unchanged because it is set by the off-world material ceiling (§17).
- **No scientific institutions** does *not* delay industrial take-off (Δ 0 yr: in this model science institutions mature only after industry) but delays the AI transition by +772 yr (paired median). Computers → D1 takes 302 yr instead of 19, and one of the 20 worlds has not reached dominance by the horizon.
- **No AI recursive research** changes the transition by +0.3 yr. Recursion is not the engine (see §16/Q1).
- **No AI resource acquisition** keeps AI-owned capital at zero (D6 = 0 by definition) and human income share at 100 %, yet delegated management still hands AI control of most capital (dominance +19 yr later).
- **HIGH vs LOW alignment** does not change *whether* or *when* machines dominate (Δ < 0.01 yr) but changes what happens to Earth: peak warming 4.0 K vs 19.9 K, human income share 0.41 vs 0.27.
- **No resource constraints**: fossil fuel never runs out and CO₂ keeps rising, so peak warming is 20.1 K against 6.9 K in the baseline. Humans end 0.56 orders of magnitude fewer (paired). In 6/20 worlds the Earth-surface economy collapses under machine dominance, and dominance arrives 22 yr earlier. The habitability rule caps heat-producing capacity but not fossil CO₂, a limitation (§18).

### S1 — Human numbers after machine dominance are decided by a pronatalist subculture, not by the machines — **EMERGENT**
- **Seed / time:** seed 1. Dominance at year 5,414; the population trough follows ~1,000 years later.
- **State before (at dominance):** 2.6·10⁸ people, pronatalist share 0.00, human income share 0.47, 291 K.
- **State after:**
  - +1,000 yr: 1.0·10⁸ people, pronatalist share 0.35.
  - +5,000 yr: 8.1·10⁹, share 0.70.
  - Horizon: **1.16·10¹⁰**.
- **Detector:** human population minimum after dominance, population at the horizon, and the `natal` share. The population-trough and population-peak detectors fire in the observer.
- **Mechanism:** Rich, low-fertility societies shrink after automation (the demographic transition needs medicine × literacy, A22a). A small subculture whose fertility ignores income is then selected: its share goes 0 → 0.71. Transfers from aligned machines (`welfareShare` × alignment) plus energy-bought food remove the Malthusian ceiling, so it regrows humanity to 10¹⁰. Machine dominance barely enters: the no-autonomy counterfactual has similar human numbers (audit C4).
- **Removing intervention:** `natal0 = 0` (no pronatalist subculture). Horizon population falls to 2.65·10⁵ (seed 1), 6.6·10⁵ (seed 42) and 3.1·10⁵ (seed 219), and every one becomes **HUMANS MARGINALISED**, a drop of 4.6–4.9 orders of magnitude in all 3/3 seeds.
- **Unpredicted?** Yes. P13 predicted "human population below its peak"; the model says the long-run human outcome is a question of cultural selection.

### S2 — Robots take over physical work years before AI controls capital or runs autonomously — **DESIGNED (timescale), reported because it falsified P9**
- **Seed / time:** seed 42. D1 at year 7,113; D5 at D1 + 1.2 yr, D3 at + 6.5 yr, D4 at + 8.5 yr (dominance certified at + 18.4 yr).
- **State before (at D1 = 0.5):** D5 < 0.5, D3 < 0.5.
- **State after (+1.2 yr):** D5 ≥ 0.5 while D3 and D4 are still < 0.5.
- **Detector:** the D1–D5 ≥ 0.5 crossing detectors. The same order appears in 30/30 previous-build seeds and 29/30 final-build seeds (one seed has D2 before D1).
- **Mechanism:**
  - Robots need only task coverage (the same capability κ as cognitive work, plus control computers) and a cost below the wage. Making robots 100× dearer delays D5 by just +1.3 yr.
  - Capital control needs two slow steps in sequence: autonomy relaxes toward its target over ~10 yr, then delegation follows over ~10 yr.
- **Removing intervention:** `autonRate = delegRate = 3/yr` (institutions adjust in months). D3 and D4 then cross at + 1.1 yr, *before* D5 (+1.2 yr), in 3/3 seeds. `robotCost` ×100 does **not** remove it.
- **Why DESIGNED:** the order is set by an assumed 10-year institutional adjustment time (A39), not by physical or economic competition.

### S3 — Alignment keeps rising after machines dominate — **EMERGENT (from designed parts), mechanism corrected**
- **Seed / time:** seed 1. Mean alignment is 0.79 at dominance (year 5,414) and 0.85 at the horizon (seed 2026: 0.78 → 0.85; seed 42: 0.66 → 0.73).
- **Detector:** population-weighted mean lineage alignment at dominance and at the horizon.
- **Mechanism (flow decomposition, seed 1):**
  - The rise is driven by residual human-controlled training.
    - Autonomy saturates at gap × reliability × (1 − regulation) ≈ **0.95**, not 1, so ~5 % of training remains under human control.
    - The training target keeps rising because human alignment research continues.
  - Market selection works in both directions: customer preference for aligned AI (`alignPref` × human income share) against the alignment tax.
  - Its net sign differs by seed (seed 1: −0.07; seed 42: +0.05).
  - My first explanation, "selected FOR by human customers", was **wrong**: `alignPref = 0` only trims the rise (0.85 → 0.81).
- **Removing intervention:** `alignSciRate = 0` (alignment science frozen). Alignment ends at 0.78 / 0.68 / 0.78 (vs 0.85 / 0.73 / 0.85), with no rise in 3/3 seeds.
- **Unpredicted?** Yes. P15 predicted alignment would *decline* after takeover (WRONG: it declined in only 13–20 % of seeds).

### S4 — A self-reinforcing decline where humans lose their customers' leverage — **EMERGENT**
- **Seed / time:** seed 1337 (final build). Dominance at year 7,441.
- **State before (at dominance):** 1.7·10⁸ people, alignment 0.58, human income share 0.46, 293 K.
- **State after:**
  - +1,000 yr: 1.9·10⁷ people, share 0.33.
  - +5,000 yr: 3.6·10⁵, share 0.23.
  - Horizon: 1.4·10⁵ people, **alignment 0.19**, share 0.07, **308 K**.
- **Detector:** mean alignment and human population at the horizon; the HUMANS MARGINALISED class. It occurs in 4/30 previous-build seeds (1337, 219, 226, 233) and 6/30 final-build seeds.
- **Mechanism (flow decomposition):**
  - The pronatalist subculture is lost, so humans stay few (S1), and the human income share falls.
  - The customer-preference bonus for aligned AI (∝ human share) then shrinks below the alignment tax (∝ autonomy), and selection drives alignment down (−0.61 over the run).
  - Less-aligned controllers put less weight on habitability in the contest that caps Earth-surface power (A21a), so heat-producing capacity on Earth grows, the Earth warms 15 K, and the human economy shrinks further.
- **Removing interventions:**
  - `alignTax = 0`: alignment 0.58 → **0.92**, temperature 291 K, humans ×3 (4.6·10⁵). The loop is broken, though humans stay marginalised because the fertility cause (S1) remains.
  - HIGH alignment toggle: alignment 0.95 → 0.51, 299 K; this only partly removes it.
- In seeds 219 and 233 the loop does not occur in the final build: the histories changed with the last-bit arithmetic. This shows the loop needs a low-fertility, low-income starting point.

## 16. POST-TAKEOVER ANALYSIS (answers to the 20 questions, from the results)

1. **What ultimately caused AI capability to accelerate?** Hardware economics and the investment loop: chip cost-performance ∝ A_comp¹⁰ with learning (min doubling time median 0.36 yr), fab capacity expanding ≤ 35 %/yr, and AI revenue pulling investment into compute. The capability-growth decomposition attributes the largest share to compute growth in 30/30 seeds before AI does 20 % of research; afterwards compute still leads in 16/30.
2. **Strongest bottleneck?** Before takeover: chip-fab capacity and *experiment compute* for algorithms (the algorithm-research bottleneck flag reads "experiment compute" through the transition). After takeover: accessible off-world material (orbital industry sits at ~0.76 of its ceiling) and, on Earth, the habitability heat budget.
3. **Intelligence or physical infrastructure?** Physical infrastructure. Removing recursive AI research changes nothing (+0.3 yr); removing semiconductor scaling removes AI; removing robots prevents physical dominance; far-future scale tracks the material ceiling 1:1 (audit C2).
4. **Was robotics necessary for machine dominance?** For *physical* dominance and the D4/D5/D6 criteria, yes (partly by definition). Without robots AI still did ~100 % of cognitive work and controlled ~90 % of capital while humans kept 62 % of income.
5. **Voluntary transfer, competition, or retained control?** Mostly voluntary-then-structural: humans delegate capital to better-returning AI managers; autonomy rises because AI work outgrows human capacity to authorise it; autonomous AI then reinvests 90 % of its income (`aiSave`), diluting human-owned capital. Humans keep substantial *income* (median share 0.33 at the horizon, via alignment-weighted transfers) but lose *authority* (human-authority index 0.12 at takeover and median 0.03 at the horizon).
6. **One decisive transition or many?** Many, but compressed: six dimensions cross 50 % in a fixed order over ~8 years, and dominance is certified 18 years after D1. The epistemic "model-calibrated" zone lasts ~2 years (audit M2).
7. **Wages and human economic relevance?** The human cognitive wage rises with automation and peaks when AI does ~100 % of cognitive work by value (median D1 at peak = 1.0), then falls in 77 % of seeds; human relevance persists through capital ownership and transfers, not wages.
8. **Centralised or fragmented?** Centralised at takeover (median 2 lineages, HHI near 1), then fragmenting (HHI falls in 30/30; lineages rise to the array cap of 12). The *direction* is a result of branching vs scale economies; the *final count* is an artifact of the cap.
9. **What limited recursive improvement?** Experiment compute (soft minimum with researchers), "ideas get harder" (alg^−0.35), the algorithmic ceiling (`algMax`), the inference floor, and capability being logarithmic in compute. Capability growth peaks and then declines in 30/30 seeds.
10. **Energy demand?** Up ×2.6·10⁵ (median) from takeover to horizon, to ~4.8·10⁷ TW (~10⁻⁷ of solar output), almost all off-world; Earth-surface use is held to median 2.4·10³ TW (range 450–25,000 TW) by the habitability rule.
11. **Humans after machine dominance?** A fertility trough (rich, low-fertility societies shrink), then — where the pronatalist subculture survived modernisation — recovery to 10⁹–4·10¹⁰; where alignment collapsed, warming and marginalisation leave 10⁴–10⁵ people. Human numbers are governed mainly by the demographic subsystem, not by machine dominance (audit C4; no-autonomy runs have similar populations).
12. **Biosphere?** Lower at the horizon than at takeover in 87 % of seeds (biosphere fraction median 0.59 at takeover → 0.41 at horizon; machine land use and warming), but never destroyed in baseline runs; the LOW-alignment runs warm ~20 K.
13. **Did AI civilisation remain on Earth?** No: ≥ 99.99 % of compute is off-world at the horizon in every takeover run.
14. **Strongest negative feedback?** Physical ceilings: accessible off-world material and Earth's radiative heat budget (enforced by aligned/human controllers). Among declared loops, N6 ("ideas get harder") dominates the AI side.
15. **What happened that was not predicted?** See §15: robots cross before capital and autonomy; alignment is selected *for*; human population rebounds through pronatalist selection; science institutions don't gate industry; recursion doesn't matter; unlimited fossil fuels cook the planet; the no-AI world boom-busts and exhausts its ores.
16. **Most variable across seeds?** Human population at the horizon: 7·10³ to 4·10¹⁰ (6.8 orders of magnitude), driven by alignment at takeover and by whether the pronatalist subculture survived.
17. **Suspiciously sensitive to arbitrary parameters?** `birthMax`, the maximum human birth rate. Lowering it by 10 % means no civilisation arises in any of 8 seeds, because pre-agricultural demography sits on a knife-edge (§17). Among AI-side parameters, the transition schedule depends on the chip cost–performance exponent `fpsExp` (−50 % doubles computers → dominance, 38 → 77 yr) and on the capability midpoint `kappa50`. The ending depends on none of the 18 swept parameters.
18. **Least convincing popular assumption?** The "intelligence explosion via recursive self-improvement": disabling AI's contribution to its own research changes the transition by 0.3 years. What mattered was hardware cost, fab/launch capacity, energy and materials.
19. **Assumption that most determined the ending?** A21: autonomy rises when AI work outgrows human capacity to authorise it. With ~10¹⁰–10²⁵ AI worker-equivalents, per-decision oversight is impossible, so autonomy → 1 and D3/D4 follow (audit C1). Combined with weak, reactive institutions (A19/A20) nothing in the model can stop it. For human fate, A21a (habitability control by aligned/human controllers) and `welfareShare` (transfers) are decisive.
20. **What would I change first?** (a) Give institutions foresight and non-oversight levers (constitutional constraints on AI goals, capital-ownership law) so "human political control with machine economic dominance" is reachable; (b) represent infrastructure ownership separately from general capital; (c) give machine lineages endogenous objectives after takeover so the far future is not "saturation at a ceiling"; (d) calibrate energy per person and population in the industrial era; (e) regulate fossil CO₂ like heat.

## 17. PARAMETER SENSITIVITY
18 parameters × ±10/25/50 % × 8 seeds (1, 42, 1337, 2026, 314159, 100, 107, 114), plus 8 baseline runs: **872 runs** (`harness/sensitivity.mjs`, `evidence/sensitivity/sweep.json`, full table in `evidence/report.md`). *Build note:* the sweep ran on the previous build (`94b20397…`, native Math). It is identical in rules and differs only in last-bit arithmetic; the final build's baseline distribution is compared with it in §22.

**Robust (a ±50 % change moves computers → dominance by < 1 yr and changes no class):**
- `oversight`, `alignTax`, `replRate`, `uncoveredEff`, `expCompute`, `welfareShare`, `safeHeatTW`, `metalTotal`, `qSlope`, `algDelta`, `contestExp`.
- `oversight` in particular is inert: the autonomy gap M/(M + oversight·H_cog) is ≈ 1 once AI work is 10⁴× human cognitive work, whatever the constant. This is audit C1 restated.

**Sensitive: timing only (dominance still in 8/8):**

| parameter | −50 % | −25 % | baseline | +25 % | +50 % | quantity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `fpsExp` (chip cost–performance exponent) | 77.0 | 47.0 | 37.7 | 34.7 | 34.8 | yr computers → dominance |
| `kappa50` (capability midpoint) | 36.8 | 37.0 | 37.7 | 41.4 | 60.5 | yr computers → dominance |
| `aiSave` | 43.0 | 38.4 | 37.7 | 37.7 | 37.7 | yr computers → dominance |
| `resDelta` (research productivity) | 7,375 | 5,153 | 3,997 | 3,588 | 2,763 | yr farming → industry |
| `birthMax` (+ side) | — | — | 3,997 | 4,921 | 5,510 | yr farming → industry |

**Sensitive: human outcomes (median horizon population, baseline 1.8·10¹⁰):**
- `contestExp` changes it monotonically: −50 % → 2.3·10¹⁰, +50 % → 1.5·10¹⁰. A sharper habitability contest heats Earth more when controllers are less aligned.
- `birthMax` +25/+50 % → 5.0/5.7·10¹⁰.
- `boostMax` and `vNeural` move it non-monotonically (0.85–3.0·10¹⁰), which looks like seed noise with n = 8.

**Suspiciously sensitive: `birthMax`.** Lowering the maximum human birth rate by only **10 %** leaves **no civilisation at all** in 8/8 seeds: hominin populations never establish (class BIOSPHERE, end population 20–33). The pre-agricultural demography sits on a knife-edge where maximum fertility barely exceeds forager mortality. This is a calibration artifact (ARTIFACT). Every "human history" in the model depends on a parameter tuned to within 10 %.

**Summary.**
- The *ending* (machine dominance) survives every ±50 % perturbation of 17 of the 18 parameters.
- The *schedule* depends mostly on hardware economics (`fpsExp`, `kappa50`).
- The *existence* of civilisation depends on a knife-edge demographic constant.
- The robustness of the ending is itself evidence for audit C1: it is structural, not tuned.

## 18. FAILURE MODES / MODEL LIMITATIONS
Declared failure modes (assumptions.md §5) and how they materialised:
1. **Encoded timing** — the κ₅₀ midpoint shifts the transition by < 20 yr for ±50 %; the *structure* (autonomy bottleneck) rather than a constant fixes the ending (audit C1).
2. **Calibration = scripting** — deep-time rates and pre-industrial research/demography were tuned (log.md, DESIGNED). Remaining gaps: pre-AI population ~3·10⁸ (real 8·10⁹); industrial energy per person ~6× too high; science institutions appear with electrification, not before industry; agrarian era ~4,000 yr (real ~10,000).
3. **Unbounded exponentials** — removed by physical ceilings, which then *set* the far-future scale (audit C2).
4. **Numerical artifacts** — pathwise agrarian timing is not converged in ε (chaotic amplification through the climate sequence; audit N6); transition timing and outcome distributions are (§17/§22). Post-takeover output in SU spikes briefly, so collapse is judged on 20-yr-smoothed output. That output counts only the Earth surface, which is why the class is named EARTH ECONOMY COLLAPSE. The orbital economy is measured separately, in energy and orbital capital.
5. **Aggregation** — one global AI capability per lineage, 12 lineages max (count saturates), 20 regions, no trade, no infrastructure ownership.
6. **Threshold artifacts** — 50 % thresholds and the 10-year persistence define "dominance"; D4 and D6 are composite indices.
7. **Visual overclaiming** — audited twice; remaining: the globe is an abstraction (regions are Voronoi cells); SU output after takeover is not a welfare measure.
8. **Noise-driven variation** — variance in human fate comes from alignment at takeover (seeded) and the pronatalist share's survival (dynamics), not from noise amplitudes.
9. **Definitional takeover** — no-autonomy and no-robotics escapes are true by definition (declared before coding).
10. **Individual seeds are not robust to last-bit arithmetic.** Moving from native to deterministic math, a change at the 10⁻¹⁶ level, changed 11 of 30 seeds' end classes (e.g. seed 42: SYMBIOTIC → plain; seed 219: EARTH ECONOMY COLLAPSED → plain). The agrarian era amplifies tiny differences through the sequence of glacial excursions. Only distributions are meaningful, never a particular seed's story. The same holds for ε/4 in pathwise convergence.
11. **Knife-edge demography.** `birthMax` −10 % → no civilisation (§17).
12. **Evidence split across two builds** (disclosed): the ablations, sensitivity sweep, ε = 0.005 run and pathwise convergence were generated on the previous build. The 30-seed baseline, surprises, determinism and capture were regenerated on the final build, and the baseline distributions agree (§12).
13. **Found in final visual inspection, not fixed:** the post-takeover panel row "compute running AI (powered × deployed)" reads 0.0 % in a machine-dominated world (`evidence/screenshots/42-post-human.png`). It measures only Earth's deployed share, while ≥ 99.99 % of compute is orbital. It is a display metric only and does not feed the rules.

Other limitations surfaced by the audits: machine conflict stays small (≤ 0.16) because lineages rarely diverge while scarce; alignment only moves under human-controlled training; human demography is largely independent of AI; fossil CO₂ is unregulated by the habitability rule; the no-AI counterfactual shows numerically sensitive boom–bust cycles.
